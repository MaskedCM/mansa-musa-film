# Storylines — Mansa Musa: The Emperor Who Crashed Gold

## 1. (Selected / Built) The Portolan Atlas Flight — One Continuous Camera Over Living Parchment
- **Device & Form:** *The object is the explanation* + *One continuous take / Flight through one world*.
- **What the viewer sees & feels:** The entire 78.6-second film unfolds as one unbroken motion-control camera flight over a single 4800×2600 hand-inked 14th-century Portolan parchment map of West Africa, the Sahara, the Mediterranean, Egypt, and Arabia. Nine historical archival prints (`01.jpg`–`09.jpg`), a merchant ledger chart, and engraved gold-and-ink hero typography drop physically onto the geographic coordinates where each event occurred, while everything non-hero dims back to `16%`.
- **First 2 seconds:** Frame 0 opens directly on the 1375 Catalan Atlas depiction of Mansa Musa (`01.jpg`) already landing over Mali (`[945, 1900]`), its golden orb radiating concentric cartographic caliper arcs as the camera pushes smoothly inward.
- **Beats:** `Hook (01 Orb in Mali) → Name (MANSA MUSA) → Empire Border (MALI) → Goldfields (02 Nuggets) → Departure (1324 + Route) → Procession (03 Caravan + 60,000) → Sahara Crossing (04 Catalan Atlas) → Arrival (05 Cairo + CAIRO) → Generosity (06 Dinar Scatter) → Market Crash (Ledger Collapse) → Aftermath (12 YEARS Stamp) → Scholars Return (07 Manuscript) → Architecture (08 Mosque + 1327) → European Fame (09 Dulcert Map) → Grand World Pull-back (1312–1337)`.
- **Look:** Aged vellum parchment (`#E8D3AC` base with 3-octave FBM paper fibre and 32-ray Portolan compass roses), iron-gall ink (`#1E150D`), and one burnished gold accent (`#E5B842`), paired with `Playfair Display` / `Palatino` serif display (`800` weight) and `JetBrains Mono` / `Consolas` monospace numbers (`800` weight).
- **Sound:** Authoritative British documentary narrator (`en-GB-RyanNeural`) as the master clock, accompanied by real HyperFrames studio SFX (`whoosh-cinematic.mp3`, `whoosh-short.mp3`, `impact-bass-1.mp3`, `impact-bass-2.mp3`, `pop.mp3`, `typing.mp3`, `riser.mp3`) landing 1 frame after each visual hit.

## 2. (Alternative A) The Cairo Merchant's Ledger — Object-to-Object Chain
- **Device & Form:** *A number lands* + *Object-to-object chain*.
- **What the viewer sees & feels:** Opens inside a Mamluk goldsmith's scale and ledger in 1324 Cairo as the price of gold suddenly plummets, then traces the chain of physical objects backward—from the devalued dinar coin to the camel saddlebags, the Taghaza salt flats, and finally the throne of Mansa Musa in Mali.
- **First 2 seconds:** A brass merchant scale slams down under a Mamluk gold dinar while a red ink line drops vertically across Damascus paper.
- **Look:** Dark walnut merchant desk (`#24170E`), Damascus ledger paper (`#EFE2C6`), crimson iron-gall ink (`#9E2A1C`), and burnished gold (`#E5B842`).
- **Sound:** Narrated with tactile scale weights, quill scratches, and deep bass impacts on each price drop.

## 3. (Alternative B) The Cartographer's Table in Majorca (1375) — Endless Zoom
- **Device & Form:** *The result first* + *An endless zoom*.
- **What the viewer sees & feels:** Begins in 1375 Majorca as Abraham Cresques lays gold leaf onto the orb in Mansa Musa's hand on the Catalan Atlas; the camera dives into the gold leaf to enter 1324 Mali, travels the pilgrimage through nested map details, and zooms back out to the completed medieval atlas at the end.
- **First 2 seconds:** Close-up of a gilded orb being burnished onto vellum before the camera dives through the gold reflection into West Africa.
- **Look:** Illuminated manuscript vellum (`#F2E6CE`), ultramarine sea pigment (`#26426B`), vermilion rhumb lines (`#B83A24`), and gold leaf (`#FFE07D`).
- **Sound:** Narrated over quiet parchment handling and measured percussive hits on each zoom threshold.
